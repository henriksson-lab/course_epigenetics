# Expected learning outcomes (draft)

A final-year MSc course on epigenetics: the biology of chromatin and DNA
methylation, the sequencing assays that measure them, and how to analyse the
data. Draft outcomes, to be aligned with the syllabus once it exists.

## Overall course outcome

By the end of the course, a student should be able to take an epigenomic
dataset (ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq or CUT&Tag, bisulfite sequencing or Hi-C), **process
it from reads to biological interpretation, and judge whether the experiment
and the analysis support the claims made**.

## Knowledge and understanding

A student can:

- explain how chromatin structure, histone modifications, DNA methylation and
  3D genome organisation regulate gene expression;
- describe the principle of ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq (and CUT&RUN/CUT&Tag),
  bisulfite sequencing and Hi-C, and the biases each assay introduces;
- explain the statistical models behind peak calling, differential
  accessibility/binding, differential methylation and Hi-C normalisation.

## Skills and abilities

A student can:

- process raw reads from an epigenomic assay to aligned, filtered reads and
  signal tracks on the command line, and assess their quality;
- call peaks, methylation levels and 3D genome features, and test for
  differences between conditions in R;
- work with genomic intervals and annotations (`bedtools`, `GenomicRanges`),
  and visualise data in a genome browser;
- integrate epigenomic data with gene expression to propose regulatory
  mechanisms.

## Assessment

An individual written exam. Exercises with code build understanding; the exam
tests reading data figures, small calculations and judging designs and claims.

## Judgement and approach

A student can:

- critically assess the design, controls, normalisation and interpretation of
  published epigenomic studies, including claims of epigenetic inheritance.

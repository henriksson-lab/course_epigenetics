Exercise: Peak calling

Goal

After this exercise you will be able to:

  • Compute MACS’s local background \(\lambda_\text{local}\) from input counts, a Poisson p-value and a fold enrichment on paper, including the depth scaling between ChIP and input.
  • Call CTCF peaks with MACS3 with and without an input, at several thresholds, and check them against ENCODE and against the CTCF motif.
  • Call a broad mark (H3K36me3) with a narrow caller, macs3 --broad and epic2, and judge the results against transcribed genes.
  • Run IDR on two CTCF replicates and on pseudoreplicates, and compute ENCODE’s rescue and self-consistency ratios.

Background

MACS with an input. Single-end reads are extended to the fragment length \(d\) and piled up. The larger library is scaled down to the size of the smaller one. At each base the pileup \(k\) is compared with \[\lambda_\text{local} = \max(\lambda_\text{BG},\ \lambda_d,\ \lambda_\text{1k},\ \lambda_\text{10k}),\] where \(\lambda_\text{BG} = N_\text{input} \times d / G\) (\(G\) the effective genome size) and \(\lambda_w\) = (input reads in a window of width \(w\) centred on the base) \(\times\, d / w\). The p-value is \(P(X \ge k)\) for \(X \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_\text{local})\); MACS converts p-scores of all bases to q-values (Benjamini–Hochberg), joins bases below the cutoff into peaks and reports the summit, the fold enrichment \((k + 1)/(\lambda + 1)\), \(-\log_{10} p\) and \(-\log_{10} q\). Without an input, MACS uses \(\lambda_\text{BG}\) and the 10-kb window of the ChIP itself.

Broad marks. SICER/epic2 divide the genome into bins (200 bp), keep bins above a lenient threshold, join them across small gaps into islands and test each island against the input. macs3 --broad links strong regions through weaker ones (--broad-cutoff).

IDR. Two replicates are called leniently; matched peaks are ranked in each; a mixture model separates a reproducible component (correlated ranks) from an irreproducible one (independent ranks). The IDR of the top \(n\) peaks is the expected fraction of irreproducible peaks among them. ENCODE compares \(N_t\) (IDR peaks from true replicates) with \(N_p\) (from pooled pseudoreplicates) and \(N_1\), \(N_2\) (each replicate’s self-pseudoreplicates): rescue ratio \(\max(N_t, N_p)/\min(N_t, N_p)\) and self-consistency ratio \(\max(N_1, N_2)/\min(N_1, N_2)\) should both be below 2.

Data

Continue in the folder of ChIP-seq: the assay, which has ctcf.final.bam, ctcf_input.final.bam, h3k36me3.final.bam and h3k36me3_input.final.bam. New here: the second replicates, gm12878_ctcf_chip_rep2.fastq.gz (ENCSR000DZN replicate 2, 36 bp, Phred+64, 463 918 reads) and gm12878_h3k36me3_chip_rep2.fastq.gz (ENCSR000AKE replicate 2, 51 bp, Phred+33, 133 285 reads), prepared by data/prepare-chip.sh. For comparison: ENCODE’s conservative IDR peaks for CTCF (gm12878_ctcf_peaks.bed.gz, 405 on chr21) and the H3K4me3 fold change (active promoters).

cd ~/epi/chip

You need macs3, idr (conda install -c bioconda idr), epic2 (conda install -c bioconda epic2 or pip install epic2) and the R packages JASPAR2024, TFBSTools and motifmatchr (from Motifs, motif enrichment and TF footprinting). The command line takes about 2 minutes.

Simplifications

Everything is on chr21 (-g 40088619), so the peak numbers are about 1–2% of genome-wide numbers, and IDR has few peaks to work with. ENCODE runs IDR on up to 300 000 lenient peaks per replicate from the whole genome.

By hand

B1. Depth scaling and λ. A ChIP has 30 M reads and its input 15 M; \(d = 200\) bp; effective genome size \(2.7 \times 10^9\). At a candidate summit the raw ChIP pileup is 36 fragments. In the input, windows centred on the summit contain: 2 reads in 200 bp, 16 reads in 1 kb and 45 reads in 10 kb.

  1. Which library is scaled, and by what factor? What is the pileup \(k\) after scaling?

  2. Compute \(\lambda_\text{BG}\) (with the input’s depth), \(\lambda_d\), \(\lambda_\text{1k}\), \(\lambda_\text{10k}\) and \(\lambda_\text{local}\).

  3. Using the table, what is the p-value? What would it be against \(\lambda_\text{BG}\) alone?

\(P(X \ge k)\) λ = 0.25 0.9 1 3.2 6
\(k\) = 10 2.1e-13 4.3e-08 1.1e-07 1.8e-03 8.4e-02
\(k\) = 14 3.4e-20 1.1e-12 4.5e-12 7.0e-06 3.6e-03
\(k\) = 18 1.8e-27 1.0e-17 6.1e-17 9.5e-09 5.7e-05
  1. What is the fold enrichment MACS reports at this summit?

B2. A shallow input. The same experiment with an input of only 1.5 M reads (one tenth). In expectation, how many input reads fall in the 200-bp, 1-kb and 10-kb windows of B1? What happens to \(\lambda_d\) when a single input read happens to fall in the 200-bp window? Why does that argue for sequencing the input at least as deep as the ChIP?

B3. With and without an input. In the course data, MACS3 finds 521 CTCF peaks with the input and 816 without. Of the 299 peaks found only without the input, 36% have a CTCF motif within 50 bp of the summit and 9% are in ENCODE’s peak set; of the 521 called with the input, 72% and 72%.

  1. Which λ does MACS use without an input, and why not a 1-kb window of the ChIP?

  2. Interpret the numbers. Are the extra peaks all false?

  3. For which kinds of samples would leaving out the input do the most damage?

B4. Which caller? Choose narrow (MACS3 default) or broad (MACS3 --broad, SICER/epic2) calling, and give a reason: (a) CTCF; (b) H3K4me3; (c) H3K27me3 in embryonic stem cells; (d) H3K36me3; (e) RNA polymerase II over gene bodies; (f) H3K9me3 over repeat-rich heterochromatin. What extra problem does (f) have?

B5. Read the figure (lecture: Real data: how many peaks are real?). The fraction of peaks with a CTCF motif falls from about 90% in the top 50 peaks to about 50% around rank 500–600; in random DNA it is 4%.

  1. Estimate how many of the 573 peaks at q < 0.1 are “real CTCF sites” if motif-less real sites are rare. What does that assume?

  2. Why is there no obvious cutoff in the curve? How does ENCODE choose the number of peaks instead?

B6. IDR. (a) Why must IDR be run on lenient peak calls (p < 0.01) and not on peaks already filtered at q < 0.01? (b) Why does IDR use ranks, not the p-values themselves? (c) Replicate 2 has half the reads of replicate 1. How does that show up in the IDR analysis and in the self-consistency ratio?

B7. ENCODE’s ratios. Three experiments:

\(N_t\) \(N_p\) \(N_1\) \(N_2\)
X 30 000 34 000 28 000 25 000
Y 8 000 31 000 26 000 20 000
Z 15 000 17 000 32 000 9 000

Compute the rescue and self-consistency ratios. Which experiments pass? What is wrong with the others?

B8. Judge the methods. A paper calls H3K27me3 peaks with MACS2 default settings (narrow) and reports “2 100 H3K27me3 peaks, median width 650 bp, mostly at CpG-island promoters”. It then compares a knockout with the wild type by the number of peaks (2 100 vs 1 300) and concludes that H3K27me3 is lost at 800 sites. Name three problems.

C1 — Process the second replicates

The chain function of ChIP-seq: the assay; define it again if you are in a new shell:

chain() {   # usage: chain <name> <reads.fastq.gz> <33 or 64>
  local s=$1
  if [ "$3" = 64 ]; then zcat $2 | perl -pe 'tr/@-~/!-_/ if $. % 4 == 0' | gzip > $s.fastq.gz
  else cp $2 $s.fastq.gz; fi
  fastp -w $T -i $s.fastq.gz -o $s.trim.fastq.gz -j $s.fastp.json -h $s.fastp.html 2> $s.fastp.log
  bowtie2 -p $T -x $D/bowtie2/hg38_chr21_chrM -U $s.trim.fastq.gz 2> $s.bowtie2.log \
    | samtools sort -@ 4 -o $s.sorted.bam -
  samtools index $s.sorted.bam
  samtools view -b -q 30 -F 1804 -o $s.q30.bam $s.sorted.bam
  samtools markdup -s $s.q30.bam $s.markdup.bam 2> $s.markdup.txt
  samtools index $s.markdup.bam
  samtools view -b -F 1024 -o $s.dedup.bam $s.markdup.bam chr21
  bedtools intersect -v -abam $s.dedup.bam -b blacklist.bed > $s.final.bam
  samtools index $s.final.bam
}

for s in ctcf ctcf_rep2 h3k36me3 h3k36me3_rep2; do
  echo $s $(samtools view -c $s.final.bam) $(grep "^READ:" $s.markdup.txt | cut -d' ' -f2) \
    $(grep "DUPLICATE SINGLE:" $s.markdup.txt | cut -d' ' -f3)
done

Explore: a Poisson peak caller

The demo runs the MACS test of the Background on simulated data: 20 kb of a 2.7-Gb genome with four true binding sites A–D (▲, strengths 100%, 30%, 55% and 15%), fragments \(d = 200\) bp. The top track shows the ChIP pileup \(k\) (brown), the input pileup (grey) and \(\lambda_\text{local}\) (blue); the strip below it shows which estimate sets \(\lambda_\text{local}\) at each base. The bottom track shows \(-\log_{10} p\) or \(-\log_{10} q\), the cutoff (red) and the called peaks (green at a true site, crimson elsewhere). The sliders set the depth of the ChIP, the depth of the input (as % of the ChIP), the signal at the strongest site (fragments per 10 M reads), the local background (× the genome average, in ChIP and input alike), a 2-kb bump of extra background in both ChIP and input (open chromatin or a copy-number gain), and the cutoff. The tick boxes switch the input and the local windows off; without an input the demo does what MACS does: \(\max(\lambda_\text{BG}, \lambda_\text{10k})\) from the ChIP itself. Drag the cursor to read the by-hand numbers at one base; the table gives them at the four sites. The q-values are Benjamini–Hochberg over the 2000 scored positions of this window (MACS uses all bases of the genome, which is stricter).

E1. The calculation of B1, live. At the start, drag the cursor to site A. Read \(k\) and the four λ values. Which one sets \(\lambda_\text{local}\), and how many input reads does it correspond to? Check the fold enrichment. Now look at site D in the table: what sets its λ, and how far above \(\lambda_\text{BG}\) is it? Untick local λ windows: what happens to the p-value at D?

E2. A bump in the background. Click open-chromatin bump (×8 in ChIP and input, input used). Are there false peaks? Put the cursor at 10.5 kb, in the bump: what are \(k\) and \(\lambda_\text{local}\), and which window sets it? Then click … without input: how many false peaks now, and what is λ at 10.5 kb? Why does the 10-kb window of the ChIP not catch the bump? Finally tick use input control again but untick local λ windows. What does this say about why MACS takes the maximum of several λ estimates?

E3. Depth and power. Click start and move ChIP reads through 5, 10, 20, 40 and 80 M (the input follows). Which sites are called at each depth? How do \(k\) and λ at site A change with depth, and what happens to the fold enrichment and to the p-value?

E4. A shallow input. Click shallow input (the input has 10% of the ChIP’s reads). What happened to the ChIP pileup and to \(\lambda_\text{BG}\)? How many sites are called now? Look at site C: what sets its λ? Compare with B2.

Solutions

E1. At site A, \(k = 65\) fragments; \(\lambda_\text{BG} = 20 \times 10^6 \times 200 / 2.7 \times 10^9 = 1.48\), \(\lambda_d = 2.00\), \(\lambda_\text{1k} = 1.60\), \(\lambda_\text{10k} = 1.76\). The 200-bp window sets \(\lambda_\text{local} = 2.00\): \(\lambda_d\) = (input reads in 200 bp) × 200/200, so two input reads. \(-\log_{10} p = 72.2\); fold enrichment \((65 + 1)/(2 + 1) = 22.0\). Site D: \(k = 14\), and four input reads happen to fall within 100 bp of it, so \(\lambda_d = 4.00\), 2.7 times \(\lambda_\text{BG}\); \(-\log_{10} p = 4.1\), \(-\log_{10} q = 2.7\), just called. Against \(\lambda_\text{BG}\) alone (local windows off), \(\lambda = 1.48\) and \(-\log_{10} p = 9.1\): a handful of input reads moves the p-value by five orders of magnitude, which is why a shallow input makes λ noisy (B2).

E2. With the input: still 4 peaks, all at true sites, no false ones. At 10.5 kb, \(k = 13\) but \(\lambda_\text{1k} = 13.0\) (13 input reads in 1 kb, × 200/1000), so \(\lambda_\text{local} = 13.0\) and \(-\log_{10} p = 0.3\): nothing. Without the input: 6 peaks, 2 of them false, inside the bump (\(-\log_{10} q\) up to 4.9); at 10.5 kb \(\lambda_\text{local} = 4.56\) (the ChIP’s 10-kb window) and \(-\log_{10} p = 3.0\). A 10-kb window averages the 2-kb bump with 8 kb of ordinary background, so it sees roughly \((8 + 2 \times 8)/10 = 2.4\) times the background, not 8 times; a 1-kb window of the ChIP would include the peak itself, which is why MACS cannot use one without an input. With the input but no local windows (\(\lambda_\text{BG}\) only), the whole bump becomes one false peak of about 2 kb (\(-\log_{10} q = 13\)). Each λ estimate catches bias at one scale: \(\lambda_d\) a narrow pile-up of input reads, \(\lambda_\text{1k}\) an open-chromatin bump, \(\lambda_\text{10k}\) a broad region such as a copy-number gain, and \(\lambda_\text{BG}\) is the floor where the input is too sparse to say anything. The maximum is the conservative choice: a base must beat the worst of them. (Note that the bump also costs sites B and C some significance: their 10-kb windows include it.)

E3. 5 M and 10 M: A, B and C are called, D is not (\(k = 3\) and 6). 20, 40 and 80 M: all four, with D at \(-\log_{10} q\) = 2.7, 8.4 and 16.4. At site A, \(k\) = 12, 31, 65, 129, 255 and \(\lambda_\text{local}\) = 1.0, 1.0, 2.0, 3.2, 5.9: both grow with the depth. The enrichment over the genome average, \(k/\lambda_\text{BG}\), stays near 40 (32, 42, 44, 44, 43); the reported fold enrichment \((k+1)/(\lambda+1)\) rises from 6.5 to 37, because at low depth the pseudocount of 1 and λ values set by single input reads weigh heavily. The p-value improves enormously (\(-\log_{10} p\) from 9 to 310). Relative Poisson noise falls as \(1/\sqrt{\lambda}\): more reads do not make a site more enriched, they make the same enrichment more certain. Weak sites need depth; the number of peaks depends on depth, so do not compare peak numbers between samples of different depth.

E4. The ChIP is scaled down to the input: its pileup is multiplied by 0.10 and rounded up, so \(k\) at A is \(\lceil 65 \times 0.1 \rceil = 7\), and \(\lambda_\text{BG} = 2 \times 10^6 \times 200 / 2.7 \times 10^9 = 0.15\). Only site A is called (\(-\log_{10} q = 6.8\)). At site C (\(k = 4\)), a single input read falls in the 200-bp window: \(\lambda_d = 1.00\), almost 7 times \(\lambda_\text{BG}\), and \(-\log_{10} p = 1.7\). With a shallow input, λ is decided by single reads, and scaling the ChIP down throws away most of its information: sequence the input at least as deeply as the ChIP (B2).

Hints

Hint 1 — λ and Poisson (B1, B2, R1)

Scale the larger library down: factor = smaller total / larger total. \(\lambda_w = n_w \times d / w\). \(\lambda_\text{local}\) is the maximum. Read \(P(X \ge k)\) from the table. MACS’s fold enrichment is \((k + 1)/(\lambda + 1)\). Everything is described in the MACS3 callpeak documentation (--slocal, --llocal, --scale-to, --bdg) and in Zhang et al. 2008. ppois(k - 1, lambda, lower.tail = FALSE) is \(P(X \ge k)\).

Hint 2 — controls and thresholds (B3, B5, C4, R2, R3)

Without a control MACS3 uses only λBG and --llocal from the ChIP (see the log line Range for calculating regional lambda). Motif scanning: motifmatchr matchMotifs, the CTCF matrix MA0139.2 in JASPAR2024. bedtools intersect -u reports each feature of -a once if it overlaps -b.

Hint 3 — broad marks (B4, B8, C5, R4)

epic2 (SICER algorithm, Zang et al. 2009): --bin-size, --gaps-allowed, --effective-genome-fraction (effective genome size / chromosome length: 40 088 619 / 46 709 983). MACS3 --broad and --broad-cutoff in the callpeak documentation. The broadPeak format is BED6+3 (UCSC). H3K9me3 domains lie largely in repeats: think about MAPQ filtering.

Hint 4 — IDR (B6, B7, C6, C7, R5)

The IDR README documents the output columns: 11 local IDR and 12 global IDR (both −log10), 15 and 19 the scores of the two replicates. Li et al. 2011 describe the model; Landt et al. 2012 and the ENCODE TF ChIP-seq pipeline the pseudoreplicate scheme. samtools view -s SEED.FRACTION subsamples reads; -U FILE writes the reads that were not selected.

Solutions — by hand

B1. (a) The ChIP is larger; it is scaled by \(15/30 = 0.5\): \(k = 18\). (b) \(\lambda_\text{BG} = 15 \times 10^6 \times 200 / 2.7 \times 10^9 = 1.11\); \(\lambda_d = 2 \times 200/200 = 2\); \(\lambda_\text{1k} = 16 \times 200/1000 = 3.2\); \(\lambda_\text{10k} = 45 \times 200/10\,000 = 0.9\). \(\lambda_\text{local} = 3.2\). (c) \(P(X \ge 18 \mid 3.2) = 9.5 \times 10^{-9}\). Against \(\lambda_\text{BG} = 1.11\) (between the 1 and the 0.9 columns) about \(10^{-17}\): eight orders of magnitude more significant, because the input shows a local excess within 1 kb. (d) \((18 + 1)/(3.2 + 1) = 4.5\).

B2. Expected input reads: \(1.5 \times 10^6 \times 200 / 2.7 \times 10^9 = 0.11\) in 200 bp, 0.56 in 1 kb, 5.6 in 10 kb. Most 200-bp windows hold 0 reads, but whenever one input read falls there, \(\lambda_d = 1 \times 200/200 = 1\), nine times \(\lambda_\text{BG}\) (0.11): the background estimate jumps up and down with single reads, and peaks near a stray input read lose significance while others do not. With too few reads the local windows estimate nothing; the input should be at least as deep as the ChIP (ENCODE requires matching depth).

B3. (a) \(\lambda_\text{BG}\) and the 10-kb window of the ChIP. A 1-kb (or \(d\)) window of the ChIP would be dominated by the peak itself and swallow it. (b) The extra peaks are a mixture: 36% have the motif (random DNA 4%), so many are real weaker CTCF sites that the conservative input-based λ (often a single input read in 100 bp) rejected; but 64% have no motif and only 9% are in ENCODE’s set, so many are noise. With the input, precision is much higher (72% motif). (c) Cancer cell lines with copy-number gains (amplified regions look enriched), weak antibodies (peaks at open promoters and hyper-ChIPable regions dominate), and broad marks (no local correction at all).

B4. (a) Narrow: point sources. (b) Narrow, or both: promoter peaks of 1–3 kb (narrow peaks plus --call-summits work well; broad gives wider regions). (c) Broad: Polycomb domains of tens of kb. (d) Broad: gene bodies. (e) Broad for the gene bodies, narrow for the promoter-proximal pausing peak; often both. (f) Broad; and most of the signal is in repeats where reads have low MAPQ, so filtering to unique reads leaves holes; such analyses keep multi-mappers or use repeat-family-level counting.

B5. (a) If every real site had the motif and noise peaks had it at the random rate (4%), the real fraction would be (motif fraction − 0.04)/(1 − 0.04). With an average motif fraction of 70% over the 573 peaks that gives \(573 \times (0.70 - 0.04)/0.96 \approx 390\) real sites. It is a lower bound: CTCF also binds some sites without a strong motif match, and some real summits are more than 50 bp from the motif. (b) Weak real sites and noise overlap in strength: the p-value distribution is continuous, there is no gap. ENCODE uses replicate consistency (IDR) to decide how far down the list to go.

B6. (a) IDR estimates the irreproducible component from the data: it needs noise peaks in the input to see where ranks stop agreeing. A pre-thresholded list contains almost only reproducible peaks; the model then cannot separate the components. (b) Ranks are comparable between replicates with different depths, signal-to-noise and score scales; the copula model only needs the order. (c) Ranks agree only as far as the weaker replicate supports peaks: the IDR curve rises earlier, \(N_t\) is limited by replicate 2. The self-pseudoreplicates of the shallow replicate give fewer peaks (\(N_2 < N_1\)), raising the self-consistency ratio.

B7. X: rescue 34/30 = 1.13, self-consistency 28/25 = 1.12: passes. Y: rescue 31/8 = 3.9 (fails), self-consistency 1.3: each replicate is fine on its own, but the two disagree much more than random halves of the pooled data: biological or technical differences between the replicates (different conditions, a failed or mislabelled replicate, a different antibody lot). Z: rescue 17/15 = 1.13, self-consistency 32/9 = 3.6 (fails): one replicate is much weaker (shallow or low enrichment) than the other.

B8. Narrow calling of a broad mark (the reported “peaks” are fragments of domains, biased to CpG islands where H3K27me3 is densest); comparing peak counts is not a differential analysis (peak numbers depend on depth, signal-to-noise and thresholds; a site can be weaker without crossing a threshold); no replicates or statistics are mentioned; a global change (a knockout of a Polycomb component may reduce H3K27me3 everywhere) needs spike-in normalisation; and “lost at 800 sites” equates absence of a peak with absence of the mark. (Any three.)

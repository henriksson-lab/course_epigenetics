Exercise: Hi-C, from reads to contact matrices

Goal

After this exercise you will be able to:

  • Write the ligation junction of a restriction enzyme, classify read pairs as valid contacts or artefacts, and bin pairs into a matrix, by hand.
  • Run one iteration of matrix balancing (ICE) and compute expected values and observed/expected on a small matrix.
  • Process real Hi-C reads on the command line: align the two ends separately with bwa mem -SP5M, parse pairs with pairtools, remove duplicates, and build and balance a .cool matrix with cooler.
  • Read Hi-C QC statistics, and compare your map with a published one in Python: balancing, \(P(s)\), and resolution against depth.

Background

Junction. A restriction enzyme with a 5′ overhang (MboI: ^GATC) leaves ends that are filled in and ligated blunt; the overhang is duplicated: GATC + GATC = GATCGATC.

Pairs. Each side of a read pair is placed by the 5′ end of its 5′-most alignment. A side is Unique (MAPQ ≥ 30), Multi-mapped, Null (unmapped), Rescued (part of a chimeric read explained by one ligation) or part of a Walk. Pair types UU, UR and RU are kept.

Artefacts. Pairs within one restriction fragment with reads pointing inward (+ −) are dangling ends; pointing outward (− +) are self-circles. At large distances the four orientations (+ −, − +, + +, − −) each make up 25%. Duplicates share both 5′ positions and strands.

Matrix. bin = ⌊position / bin size⌋; pixel (i, j) counts pairs with one side in bin i and one in bin j.

Balancing (ICE). Model \(O_{ij} = b_i b_j T_{ij}\); iterate \(b_i \leftarrow\) row sum\(_i\) / mean row sum, divide pixel (i, j) by \(b_i b_j\), until row sums are equal. Expected \(E(s)\) = mean of the balanced diagonal at distance \(s\); O/E = pixel / \(E(s)\).

You need bwa, samtools, pairtools (1.1), cooler (0.10) and, in Python, cooler, cooltools, numpy, pandas and matplotlib (one conda line: conda create -n hic -c conda-forge -c bioconda python=3.11 cooler=0.10 cooltools=0.7 pairtools=1.1 bwa samtools bedtools pybigwig pyfaidx bioframe matplotlib). The command-line part takes about 10 minutes with 8 threads; on a cluster, wrap C2 in a SLURM job (sbatch -c 8 --mem 8G --wrap "...").

Simplification: the reads were selected by aligning 30 million read pairs to the whole genome and keeping those with a side on chr21 (the other side on chr21 or unplaced), so you can align to chr21 only. Contacts with other chromosomes are absent, and a few reads that are multi-mapped in the whole genome look unique on chr21 alone. The whole-genome statistics are in gm12878_hic_fullgenome_stats.txt.

By hand

B1. Junctions and fragments.

  1. HindIII cuts A^AGCTT, leaving a 5′ overhang AGCT. Write the junction after fill-in and blunt ligation. Do the same for NcoI (C^CATGG).

  2. In random sequence with equal base frequencies, how often does a 4-bp site occur? A 6-bp site? How many MboI sites do you expect on chr21 (40.1 Mb of sequence)?

  3. Why can a 6-cutter map not reach 1-kb resolution, whatever the depth?

B2. A chimeric read. A 101-bp read 1 has the junction GATCGATC starting at position 41 (1-based). Its first 44 bases align to chr21:20 000 000 (+ strand), the rest to chr21:34 500 120 (+ strand). Read 2 aligns to chr21:34 500 600 on the − strand.

  1. Which positions represent the two sides of the pair?

  2. Why is the pair “rescued” (UR) rather than a walk?

  3. What would a normal paired-end aligner (without -SP) have done with this pair?

B3. Classify pairs. MboI sites near the loci are at chr21:1 000 000, 1 000 310, 1 000 650 and 1 001 020. Classify each pair (side 1 always the leftmost):

pair pos1 strand1 pos2 strand2
p1 1 000 020 + 1 000 290 −
p2 1 000 030 − 1 000 280 +
p3 1 000 100 + 1 000 600 −
p4 1 000 400 − 1 250 000 −
p5 1 000 400 − 1 250 000 −
p6 1 000 700 + (chr19) 2 000 000 +

B4. Bin and count. With 100-kb bins, fill the upper triangle of a 4 × 4 matrix for chr21:0–400 kb from these pairs: (15 000, 80 000), (120 000, 390 000), (210 000, 260 000), (230 000, 280 000), (50 000, 330 000), (305 000, 395 000), (110 000, 190 000), (99 999, 100 000).

B5. One ICE iteration. A balanced map must have equal row sums. Raw matrix:

      a    b    c
a    10    8    2
b     8   20    6
c     2    6    4

  1. Compute the row sums and their mean, the bias \(b_i\) of each bin, and the matrix after one iteration (divide pixel (i, j) by \(b_i b_j\)).

  2. Compute the new row sums. Are they closer to equal?

  3. Bin c had 4 % of the reads in the previous library and 12 % in this one, which has the same cell type. What could cause that, and does balancing fix it?

B6. Expected and O/E. A balanced 5-bin map (upper triangle, diagonal excluded):

      1     2     3     4     5
1     –   0.20  0.08  0.05  0.06
2           –   0.22  0.07  0.03
3                 –   0.18  0.09
4                       –   0.20
  1. Compute \(E(s)\) for \(s\) = 1, 2 and 3 bins. (b) Compute O/E for pixels (1, 3), (3, 5), (1, 4) and (1, 5). Which pixel is most enriched? (c) Is the pixel with the highest balanced value the most interesting?

B7. Read the QC. Two libraries from the same cells, 50 M read pairs each:

library A library B
UU + UR + RU 78% 74%
duplicates (of mapped) 4% 38%
cis among unique pairs 84% 52%
cis > 20 kb among unique pairs 55% 21%
orientations converge at 1.5 kb 2 kb
  1. Which library would you sequence deeper? (b) What do B’s duplicate rate and cis fraction suggest about what went wrong?

B8. Depth for resolution. A Hi-C library gives 400 million valid contacts genome-wide (3.1 Gb), 75% cis.

  1. How many 5-kb bins are there in the genome? On average, how many contacts touch each bin? (Each contact counts for two bins.)

  2. Rao et al.’s criterion asks for ≥ 1 000 contacts in 80% of bins. Is 5 kb realistic? 25 kb?

C2 — Align and parse

bwa mem -SP5M -t 8 chr21.fa $D/gm12878_hic_R1.fastq.gz $D/gm12878_hic_R2.fastq.gz 2> bwa.log \
  | pairtools parse --min-mapq 30 --walks-policy 5unique --max-inter-align-gap 30 --drop-sam --drop-seq \
      -c chr21.sizes --output-stats parse.stats -o chr21.pairs.gz
zcat chr21.pairs.gz | grep -v '^#' | head -5
zcat chr21.pairs.gz | grep '^#columns'
grep -E '^(total|total_unmapped|total_single_sided_mapped|total_mapped|cis_1kb\+)\s' parse.stats
grep '^pair_types' parse.stats

  1. What are the columns of the .pairs file? Which column holds the pair type?

  2. How many pairs have both sides mapped? Which pair types are most common?

  3. How long did the alignment take? (time in front of the command, or the timestamps in bwa.log.)

C3 — Sort, deduplicate, select

pairtools sort --nproc 4 -o chr21.sorted.pairs.gz chr21.pairs.gz
pairtools dedup --max-mismatch 3 --output-stats dedup.stats --output-dups chr21.dups.pairs.gz \
  -o chr21.nodups.pairs.gz chr21.sorted.pairs.gz
pairtools select '(pair_type == "UU") or (pair_type == "UR") or (pair_type == "RU")' \
  -o chr21.valid.pairs.gz chr21.nodups.pairs.gz
grep -E '^(total_mapped|total_dups|total_nodups|cis|cis_1kb\+|cis_20kb\+)\s' dedup.stats
zcat chr21.valid.pairs.gz | grep -vc '^#'
  1. What fraction of mapped pairs are duplicates? (b) How many valid contacts remain? What fraction are more than 1 kb and more than 20 kb apart?

C4 — Whole-genome QC from the prepared statistics

  2. In the dist_freq lines, compare the four orientations at short distances (178–237 bp, 316–422 bp) and at 1.8–2.4 kb and 32–42 kb. Where do they become equal?

import re, numpy as np, pandas as pd, matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from matplotlib.colors import LogNorm
import cooler, cooltools
D = "/path/to/course/data"

P1 — The orientation plot

st = pd.read_csv(f"{D}/gm12878_hic_fullgenome_stats.txt", sep="\t", header=None, index_col=0)[1]
rows = [(int(m.group(1)), m.group(2), float(v)) for k, v in st.items()
        if (m := re.match(r"dist_freq/(\d+)-?(?:\d+|\+)?/(..)$", k))]
dfq = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["lo", "strand", "n"]).pivot(index="lo", columns="strand", values="n")
dfq = dfq[(dfq.index >= 1) & (dfq.index < 1e6)]
frac = dfq.div(dfq.sum(axis=1), axis=0)
print((frac * 100).round(1).iloc[::3])
frac.plot(logx=True, marker="o", ms=3); plt.axhline(0.25, ls="--", color="grey")
plt.xlabel("distance (bp)"); plt.ylabel("fraction"); plt.savefig("p1_orientation.png", dpi=120)
  1. Which orientation dominates below 1 kb, and which artefact is it? Above which distance are all four within 0.03 of 0.25?

P2 — Raw and balanced

c = cooler.Cooler("chr21.mcool::resolutions/250000")
raw = c.matrix(balance=False).fetch("chr21").astype(float)
bal = c.matrix(balance=True).fetch("chr21")
w = c.bins().fetch("chr21")["weight"]
print(len(w), "bins;", w.isna().sum(), "masked")
cov = raw.sum(axis=0)
print("raw row sums of unmasked bins: min", cov[w.notna()].min(), "max", cov[w.notna()].max())
far = np.abs(np.subtract.outer(np.arange(len(w)), np.arange(len(w)))) >= 2   # balancing ignores 2 diagonals
print("balanced row sums:", np.round(np.nansum(np.where(far, bal, 0), axis=0)[w.notna()][:5], 3))
fig, axs = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(11, 5))
for ax, M, t in zip(axs, [raw, bal], ["raw", "balanced"]):
    M = np.where(M > 0, M, np.nan)
    ax.imshow(M, cmap="Reds", norm=LogNorm(vmin=np.nanpercentile(M, 5), vmax=np.nanpercentile(M, 99.5))); ax.set_title(t)
plt.savefig("p2_balance.png", dpi=120)
  1. How many bins are masked, and where are they? (b) How much do raw row sums vary? What are the balanced row sums?

P3 — Distance decay: ours against ENCODE’s

v = pd.DataFrame({"chrom": ["chr21"], "start": [0], "end": [46_709_983], "name": ["chr21"]})
for lab, fn in [("ours", "chr21.mcool::resolutions/10000"),
                ("ENCODE", f"{D}/gm12878_hic_chr19_chr21.mcool::resolutions/10000")]:
    c = cooler.Cooler(fn)
    e = cooltools.expected_cis(c, view_df=v, nproc=2)
    e["s"] = e.dist * 10000
    e = e[(e.dist >= 2) & (e.s <= 2e7)]
    plt.loglog(e.s, e["balanced.avg"] / e["balanced.avg"].sum(), label=lab)
    sel = (e.s >= 1e5) & (e.s <= 1e6)
    print(lab, "slope 100 kb-1 Mb:", round(np.polyfit(np.log10(e.s[sel]), np.log10(e["balanced.avg"][sel]), 1)[0], 2))
plt.legend(); plt.xlabel("s (bp)"); plt.ylabel("P(s)"); plt.savefig("p3_ps.png", dpi=120)
  1. Compare the two slopes. (b) Why does \(P(s)\) need so few reads while loops need billions?

P4 — Resolution against depth

for res in [10000, 50000, 100000, 250000]:
    out = []
    for fn in [f"chr21.mcool::resolutions/{res}", f"{D}/gm12878_hic_chr19_chr21.mcool::resolutions/{res}"]:
        m = cooler.Cooler(fn).matrix(balance=False, sparse=True).fetch("chr21")
        cov = np.asarray(m.sum(axis=1)).ravel()          # symmetric matrix: row sum = contacts of the bin
        cov = cov[cov > 0]
        out.append(round(np.mean(cov >= 1000) * 100, 1))
    print(f"{res // 1000:>4} kb: ours {out[0]:5.1f}%  ENCODE {out[1]:5.1f}% of bins with >= 1000 contacts")
  1. What is the resolution of our map, and of ENCODE’s, by Rao et al.’s 80% criterion? (b) What does this mean for which features you can analyse with our map?

P5 — Observed/expected on our map

c = cooler.Cooler("chr21.mcool::resolutions/250000")
e = cooltools.expected_cis(c, view_df=v, nproc=1).set_index("dist")["balanced.avg"]
m = c.matrix(balance=True).fetch("chr21")
n = m.shape[0]
d = np.abs(np.subtract.outer(np.arange(n), np.arange(n)))
oe = m / e.reindex(np.arange(n)).values[d]
plt.figure(figsize=(6, 5.5))
plt.imshow(np.log2(oe), cmap="coolwarm", vmin=-1.5, vmax=1.5); plt.colorbar(label="log2 O/E")
plt.title("our chr21, 250 kb, O/E"); plt.savefig("p5_oe.png", dpi=120)
print("mean O/E 2, 10 and 40 bins from the diagonal:", [round(np.nanmean(np.diag(oe, k)), 2) for k in (2, 10, 40)])
  1. Why is the mean O/E along each diagonal 1? (b) Can you see compartments in our 250-kb map? Compare with Calling compartments, TADs and loops.

Hints

Hint 1 — junctions, pairs and artefacts (B1–B3)

Fill-in copies the 4-nt overhang onto the other end, so the junction is site-up-to-cut + overhang + overhang + rest of site. Probability of a \(k\)-mer at a position is \(4^{-k}\) (count one strand for a palindromic site). The two sides of a pair are the 5′ ends of the 5′-most alignment of each read. A pair is an artefact if both sides fall between the same two restriction sites (or adjacent fragments) with inward (+ −) or outward (− +) reads. Duplicates match at both sides and strands. See Lajoie et al. 2015 and the pairtools documentation on pair types.

Hint 2 — binning, ICE and O/E (B4–B6, B8)

bin = ⌊pos / 100 000⌋; order the two bins so i ≤ j. For ICE: \(b_i\) = row sum\(_i\) / mean of the row sums, new pixel = old pixel / (\(b_i b_j\)). \(E(s)\) = mean of one diagonal. Imakaev et al. 2012 for ICE; for resolution, Rao et al. 2014.

Hint 3 — command line (C1–C6)

bwa mem options -S (skip mate rescue), -P (skip pairing), -5 (5′-most split alignment primary), -M (mark shorter splits secondary). pairtools parse, sort, dedup, select; the .pairs format; pairtools stats output. cooler cload pairs, zoomify, balance, dump. A .mcool resolution is addressed as file.mcool::resolutions/10000.

Hint 4 — Python (P1–P5)

The stats file is two columns, key and value. cooler API: matrix(balance=False) raw counts, balance=True balanced, sparse=True a SciPy sparse matrix; bins() the bin table with weight. cooltools.expected_cis: dist in bins, balanced.avg is \(E(s)\). np.subtract.outer gives the distance of every pixel from the diagonal.

Solutions

Solutions — by hand

B1. (a) HindIII: A + AGCT (filled in) + AGCT + T → AAGCTAGCTT. NcoI: C + CATG + CATG + G → CCATGCATGG. (b) \(4^{-4}\): once every 256 bp; \(4^{-6}\): once every 4 096 bp (both sites are palindromes, so count one strand). chr21: 40.1 × 10⁶ / 256 ≈ 157 000 MboI sites expected (C1 counts the real number). (c) A contact is only resolved to the restriction fragments it joins; with fragments of ~4 kb on average (and many much longer), contacts within or between neighbouring fragments are blurred over several kb, and bins of 1 kb would often contain no cut site at all.

B2. (a) Side 1: the 5′ end of read 1’s first alignment, chr21:20 000 000 (+). Side 2: the 5′ end of read 2, which on the − strand is its rightmost base, near chr21:34 500 700 (the reported position is the 5′ end). (b) The 3′ part of read 1 and read 2 both map near 34.5 Mb, on the same fragment and consistent with one molecule: one ligation explains everything, so the chimeric part adds no new contact. A walk would need a third locus. (c) It would expect the mates to face each other a few hundred bp apart: it might rescue read 2 by forcing a local alignment near 20 Mb, mark the pair improper, or choose a poor alignment of the 3′ part, losing or corrupting the contact.

B3. p1: both sides in the fragment 1 000 000–1 000 310, inward (+ −): dangling end. p2: same fragment, outward (− +): self-circle. p3: + − inward across one site (1 000 310) into the next fragment, 500 bp: re-ligation of neighbours (or a dangling end over an uncut site), not a useful contact. p4: different fragments 250 kb apart: valid cis contact. p5: identical to p4 at both sides and strands: duplicate of p4, removed. p6: valid trans contact (chr21–chr19).

B4. Bins (i, j): (0, 0), (1, 3), (2, 2), (2, 2), (0, 3), (3, 3), (1, 1), (0, 1) — the last one because 99 999 is in bin 0 and 100 000 in bin 1. Upper triangle:

      0   1   2   3
0     1   1   0   1
1         1   0   1
2             2   0
3                 1

B5. (a) Row sums 20, 34, 12; mean 22. Biases \(b\) = 0.909, 1.545, 0.545. New matrix: (a, a) = 10/0.826 = 12.1; (a, b) = 8/1.405 = 5.69; (a, c) = 2/0.496 = 4.03; (b, b) = 20/2.388 = 8.38; (b, c) = 6/0.843 = 7.12; (c, c) = 4/0.298 = 13.4. P5. (a) \(E(s)\) is defined as the mean of each diagonal, so dividing by it makes the mean O/E 1 on every diagonal (1.0 at 2, 10 and 40 bins; distances 0 and 1 are not used). (b) Yes, noisily: a checkerboard of red and blue blocks from about 14 Mb on, the same pattern as the chr21 eigenvector in Calling compartments, TADs and loops (mostly B from 16 to 32 Mb, A from 32 to 37 and 41 to 46 Mb). Compartments need few reads; this is why they were discovered first, with 1-Mb maps.

Where this leads

  • Calling compartments, TADs and loops starts from a balanced map and its expected values, as built here, and uses ENCODE’s deep map to call features.
  • The full pipeline for a whole Hi-C or Micro-C experiment is run with distiller, nf-core/hic or Juicer on a cluster; the steps are the ones of C1–C5.
  • Pairs files are also the input for single-cell Hi-C and for multi-way contact methods (Pore-C), which keep walks rather than reducing them to pairs.
